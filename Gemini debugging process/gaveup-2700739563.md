## 5. Root Cause Analysis: Why `p1@p2` and `both@spectator` Gave Up

### The Focus Sash Non-Activation Anomaly
1. In the ground-truth mimic battle, P1's Basculegion held a Focus Sash.
2. On Turn 4, Kingambit's Life Orb Sucker Punch rolled 218 damage against Basculegion's 219 HP. Basculegion survived on 1 HP by raw physical bulk.
3. Because Basculegion survived with 1 HP naturally, its Focus Sash was not consumed. The observed channel log recorded:
   ```
   |move|p2a: Kingambit|Sucker Punch|p1a: Basculegion
   |-supereffective|p1a: Basculegion|1
   |-damage|p1a: Basculegion|1/100
   ```
   There was **no** `|-enditem|p1a: Basculegion|Focus Sash` line.

### The Default and Usage Guess Trap
In `infer.mjs`, when inferring P1's team (`p1@p2` or `both@spectator`), candidate guesses for Basculegion start from either:
- The default spread: `{ hp: 0, atk: 32, def: 2, spa: 0, spd: 0, spe: 32 }` (HP 195, Def 87).
- Popular Smogon usage spreads: e.g., `2/32/0/0/0/32` (HP 197, Def 85) or `0/32/2/0/0/32` (HP 195, Def 87).

Against any spread with low HP and Def investment (such as 0–4 HP and 0–2 Def):
- Kingambit's Sucker Punch deals **245 to 296 damage**.
- Because max HP is only 195–199, **100% of damage rolls (16/16) deal lethal damage**.
- When a hit deals lethal damage to a full-HP Pokémon holding a Focus Sash, Showdown's simulator **always triggers Focus Sash**.
- In Showdown's protocol, Focus Sash triggers before the damage line:
  ```
  |-supereffective|p1a: Basculegion
  |-enditem|p1a: Basculegion|Focus Sash
  |-damage|p1a: Basculegion|1/195
  ```

### The Reconstruct Divergence
When `reconstruct` runs with any standard guess:
- Line expected by observed protocol: `|-damage|p1a: Basculegion|1/100`
- Line produced by rebuild simulation: `|-enditem|p1a: Basculegion|Focus Sash`

The reconstructor flags a protocol divergence on Turn 4:
```json

  "index": 2,
  "expected": "|-damage|p1a: Basculegion|1/100",
  "actual": "|-enditem|p1a: Basculegion|Focus Sash"

```
`reconstruct` assumes this mismatch is caused by an erroneous choice or an incorrect RNG seed on Turn 4. It launches an exhaustive backtracking search over all legal choices and dice ordinals. However, because lethality is determined entirely by Basculegion's stat points (which are constant during a single reconstruct pass), **no choice or die roll can prevent Focus Sash from popping**.
A single reconstruct pass on Turn 4 spends its entire probe budget (thousands of probes, 30–70 seconds per call) and fails.

### The Evidence Pass Blind Spot

1. `built.inputLog` only reproduces through Turn 3.
2. In `evidencePass`, the simulation runs using `built.inputLog`. The cutoff occurs at Turn 4.
3. In `evidence.mjs`:

4. As a result, the evidence pass does **not** evaluate Turn 4 damage as a verified event and does **not** narrow Basculegion's HP or Def candidate space.
5. Basculegion retains **136,655,945 spreads** (99.995% of its total initial pool).

### The Fallback Single-Dimension Failure
When `infer.mjs` detects repeated rebuild failures on Turn 4, it invokes `fallback`:
1. Fallback iterates through each stat dimension of Basculegion (`hp`, `atk`, `def`, `spa`, `spd`, `spe`) **one stat at a time**:
   - Testing `hp = 32` (with current Def = 0–2): Sucker Punch deals 251–296 damage against 227 HP. It is still 100% lethal. Focus Sash still pops (`|-enditem` divergence).

2. To survive Sucker Punch without popping Focus Sash AND land on `1/100` HP, Basculegion requires a **co-dependent pair of stats** (e.g., HP 24 AND Def 14).
3. Fallback never searches pairs of stats simultaneously. Every single-stat probe fails.
4. Fallback logs `nothing gets past the line` and marks the failing line as fruitless.
