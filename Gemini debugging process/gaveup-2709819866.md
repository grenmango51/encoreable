### D. The Local Search Trap & Dimensionality Explosion

1. Perturbing P1 Basculegion $\implies$ fails on Turn 1 lines.

## 7. Contrast: View Asymmetry & Architectural Recommendations

### Why `p1@p2` and `p2@p1` Succeeded Easily
1. **Single-Side Search Space**:
   - In `p1@p2`, Player 2 is fully known (`known[1] = true`). All stats, abilities, items, and exact HP values for Golisopod, Pelipper, Rillaboom, and Basculegion are fixed constants. The solver only searches Player 1's Stat Points, reducing the search space from $136.7\text{M}^{8}$ down to $136.7\text{M}^{4}$.
   - Similarly, in `p2@p1`, Player 1's stats are completely anchored.
2. **Elimination of Cross-Side Coupling**:
   Because Player 2's Golisopod Defense and HP are fixed constants in `p1@p2`, the damage dealt by Basculegion's Wave Crash is a monotonic function of Basculegion's Attack and PRNG damage rolls. The solver does not have to simultaneously adjust opposing defense spreads.
3. **Exact Integer Feedback**:
   Channel 1 and Channel 2 report exact integer HP values for the player's own Pokémon, eliminating percentage rounding ambiguity and allowing the solver to immediately prune incompatible damage rolls.

### Why `both@spectator` Stalled
In `both@spectator`, neither player is known, and all HP values on Channel 0 are rounded percentages.
The solver is forced to navigate a coupled 42-dimensional parameter space where:
- Damage dealt in Turn 5 depends on the ratio $\frac{\text{Atk}_{\text{P1}}}{\text{Def}_{\text{P2}}}$.
- Recoil depends on $\lfloor \frac{\text{Damage}}{3} \rfloor$ relative to $\text{MaxHP}_{\text{P1}}$.
- The same $\text{Atk}_{\text{P1}}$, $\text{Def}_{\text{P1}}$, and $\text{HP}_{\text{P1}}$ must simultaneously satisfy Turn 1's resisted Wave Crash damage and recoil.
- Because `fallback` in `infer.mjs` only perturbs one Pokémon at a time, it is mathematically incapable of finding the joint saddle-point solution across opposing Pokémon.

### Architectural Findings & Recommendations
1. **Multi-Parameter Coordinate Descent for Coupled Damage/Recoil**:
   When a fallback failure occurs on a recoil line (`|-damage|...|[from] Recoil`), fallback should identify both the attacking Pokémon and the defending target, and test joint 2-point perturbations (adjusting the attacker's Atk/HP and the defender's Def/HP simultaneously) rather than perturbing only one Pokémon in isolation.
2. **Cross-Turn Recoil Tolerance Relaxation in Early Restarts**:
   Recoil percentages on Channel 0 are prone to $\pm 1\%$ rounding discrepancies depending on integer division truncation. If a simulation reproduces all move choices and faints through Turn 5 with only a $1\%$ recoil difference (`31/100` vs `30/100`), the reconstruction engine should allow forward exploration to continue to Turn 6 and Turn 7 to collect subsequent move evidence, rather than treating Turn 5 as a hard blocking failure and expending 107 rebuilds in an uncoordinated loop.
3. **Pruning Dead Benched Pokémon from Fallback**:
   In Phase 6, fallback on Turn 5 spent over 111 seconds testing Stat Points on `Pelipper` (which had already fainted in Turn 1) and `Incineroar` (which was benched and took no damage). Fallback candidates for a stalled turn $T$ should be strictly restricted to Pokémon active on the field during turn $T$ or those who directly influenced damage calculations in turn $T$.
