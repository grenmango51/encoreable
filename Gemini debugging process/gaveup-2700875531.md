## 6. Comparison: Single-View vs. Spectator Inference

### The Asymmetric Rigidity Trap in `p2@p1`
- In `p2@p1` (Channel 1, P1 known), Player 1's team is completely fixed down to every Stat Point.
- Furthermore, the observer sees exact integer HP on all hits taken and dealt by Player 1.
- While this usually provides powerful constraints that accelerate convergence in simple battles, here it creates an **over-constrained rigidity trap**:
  - P1 Sneasler, P1 Basculegion, and P1 Kingambit have fixed defenses and HP.
  - P2's attacking Pokémon must roll damage values that hit exact discrete integers on P1's targets across multiple simultaneous turns.
  - Simultaneously, P2's defending Pokémon must survive/faint at exact discrete integer thresholds.
  - When fallback tries to adjust P2's stats to fix a Turn 3 or Turn 4 divergence, mutating P2's Stat Points instantly cascades backwards, breaking Turn 1 or Turn 2 damage.
  - Because Player 1 cannot flex a single Stat Point, fallback exhausts its coordinate mutations and cycles through all search tiers until timing out after 1,934.6s.

### Why `both@spectator` Succeeded in 187.6 Seconds
- In `both@spectator`, neither player's stats are anchored.
- The simulator is free to explore joint assignments where both the attacker's Stat Points and the defender's Stat Points are adjusted in concert.
- The loose percentage quantization creates overlapping solution regions for damage rolls.
- Although Turn 1 required 4 fallback rebuilds and 78.9s to reconcile the Sneasler speed-tie and damage rolls, once candidate bounds were updated and Search 2 (`usage` tier) launched, the search found compatible witness spreads that effortlessly satisfied Turns 2 through 6 in just 4 rounds (rounds 5 to 8), completing the proof in 187.6s.
