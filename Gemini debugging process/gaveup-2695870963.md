## 5. Root Cause Analysis: Why `both@spectator` Stalled on Turn 1 (`1.8`)

To pass Turn 1 (`1.0` and `1.8`), the inference must satisfy the **three-way chain inequality**:
$$\text{Spe}(\text{p1b: Ninetales}) \ge \text{Spe}(\text{p1a: Raichu}) \ge \text{Spe}(\text{p2b: Raichu})$$
which reduces in SP terms (because Ninetales has base `109` Spe vs. Raichu's base `110` Spe, all three with `+Spe` natures) to:
$$\text{Ninetales}_{\text{spe}} \ge \text{Raichu}_{1,\text{spe}} + 1 \quad \text{and} \quad \text{Raichu}_{1,\text{spe}} \ge \text{Raichu}_{2,\text{spe}}$$
(with `Raichu1_spe = 31` tying `Ninetales_spe = 32` at `177`). Every single search failed to find this due to **three interacting bugs** in `speed.mjs` and `infer.mjs`:

### A. `speed.mjs` `between()` Fails to Read `|cant|...|flinch` When Preceded by a Higher-Priority Move (`Fake Out`)
In `round 1` of all 6 starts, `reconstruct` stopped at `1.8` (`wanted |cant|p1b: Ninetales|flinch`, `got |move|p1a: Raichu|Rising Voltage|p2a: Gholdengo`). When `evidencePass` ran `speedRules()` (`scripts/lib/inference/speed.mjs` lines 702–786):
1. `was` parsed `|cant|p1b: Ninetales|flinch` (`P = Ninetales`). Because `Ninetales` flinched and never executed a move on Turn 1, `chosen()` (line 709) returned `null`, setting **`unseen = true`** (line 746).
2. With `unseen = true`, `opened(item, between)` (line 731) relies on `between(item)` (lines 741–745) to verify that `Ninetales` acted in the same `(order, priority)` bracket as `Q` (`p1a: Raichu`'s `0`-priority `Rising Voltage`):
   ```js
   const before = x ? executed[executed.indexOf(ex) - 1] : null;
   const a = before?.sort ...
   const between = item => !!a && a.order === x.order && a.priority === x.priority && ...
   ```
3. However, the action executed immediately before `p1a: Raichu`'s `Rising Voltage` (`x`, `priority = 0`) was **`p2b: Raichu`'s `Fake Out` (`a`, `priority = +3`)**!
4. Because `a.priority === x.priority` (`3 === 0`) is **`false`**, `between` returned `false`, leaving `y = null` and `P = null`.
5. Consequently, `evidencePass` emitted **no speed constraint** (`Ninetales >= p1a: Raichu`), `next` stayed identical to `picks`, and `inferSpreads` handed the entire problem to `fallback` with `[0..32]` Speed still open on all leads. *(Note: Even though `Ninetales` had `Protect` (`+4`), flinching after `+3` `Fake Out` already ruled out `+4` `Protect`, proving `Ninetales` queued a `0`-priority move — yet `between` has no way to see that when `Q` is the first move of the `0`-priority bracket.)*

### B. Starts 2 & 5 (`usage` Start): The `disputed` $\to$ `done.has()` Bug Killed Binary Halving on `p1a: Raichu spe`
In Starts 2 and 5 (`usage` start), `p2b: Raichu` was already initialized at `#1 Timid` (`spe = 23`, **`Spe = 168`**), while `Ninetales` was at `spe = 32` (**`Spe = 177`**) and `p1a: Raichu` was at `spe = 32` (**`Spe = 178`**). Any `p1a: Raichu spe` in `[23..31]` (`Spe 168..177`) immediately satisfies **both** `1.0` and `1.8`! Yet `fallback` missed it in both Start 2 (`8` rebuilds) and Start 5 (`32` rebuilds):
1. First, `fallback`'s `disputed` pair loop (`infer.mjs` lines 761–786) probed **`{Ninetales spe 32, p1a: Raichu spe 0}`** (lines 29 & 143). Because `Ninetales` was *already* at `spe = 32`, the resulting spread vector had the **exact same signature** as `{p1a: Raichu spe 0}` alone (`p1a` = `143 < 168` $\to$ stopped at `1.0`, `'early'`).
2. **`probe()` added that signature to `done` (line 675), but `disputed` never populated `db.sides` (`d.sides` for `p1a: Raichu spe`).**

## 7. Single-View vs. Spectator Contrast & Architectural Takeaways

### Why `p2@p1` and `p1@p2` Passed Cleanly
- **In `p1@p2` (Channel 2, `p2` Known)**: `p2b: Raichu`'s pre-Mega **Spe = 149** (`spe = 6`) is a known constant. When `fallback` probes `p1a: Raichu spe = 16` (`Spe 160`) in Round 1, it simultaneously stays above `149` (`1.0` passes) and drops below `p1b: Ninetales`'s `177` (`1.8` passes), clearing Turn 1 in a single probe.
- **In `p2@p1` (Channel 1, `p1` Known)**: `p1b: Ninetales` (**Spe 167**) and `p1a: Raichu` (**Spe 163**) are known constants, so `1.8` (`Ninetales` flinching before `p1a: Raichu`) is satisfied unconditionally on every guess, and `1.0` (`p1a: Raichu 163 >= p2b: Raichu`) is a simple single-Pokémon Speed bound on `p2b: Raichu` (`spe <= 19`), alongside Channel 1's exact `130/149` HP printout.

### Architectural Takeaways (`scripts/lib/inference/infer.mjs` & `speed.mjs`)
1. **Allow `escort()` to Coordinate Same-Turn Earlier Order Failures (`infer.mjs` line 728)**:
   Changing `!(stop.turn < t)` to `!(stop.turn < t || (stop.turn === t && stop.index < diff.index))` in `escort()` immediately unlocks two-Pokémon Speed chains within the same turn — such as a Mega Evolution order (`1.0`) followed by a move order (`1.8`). When lowering `p1a: Raichu spe` breaks `1.0` against `p2b: Raichu`, `escort` would move `p2b: Raichu spe` to `0`, immediately clearing Turn 1 in Start 1.
2. **Record `disputed` Probe Outcomes (or Don't Swallow `done.has()` as `null`) Before `halving` (`infer.mjs` lines 675, 746, 777)**:
   When `disputed` probes `{Ninetales spe 32, p1a: Raichu spe 0}` and `Ninetales` is already at `32`, `done` caches that signature without populating `p1a: Raichu`'s `d.sides`. Later, `tryValue(p1a: Raichu spe, 0)` gets `null` from `probe()` instead of `'early'`, silently disabling `halving` on `p1a: Raichu spe` in Starts 2 & 5. Either letting `disputed` go through a helper that records single-dim moves in `d.sides` (or storing outcomes in `done` keyed by signature instead of `null`) would have let Start 2 (`usage`) solve Turn 1 in ~5 rebuilds.
3. **Verify `unseen` Move Priority Using Earlier Brackets in `speed.mjs` (`between()` at line 744)**:
   When `Ninetales` flinched (`unseen = true`) at `1.8`, `p1a: Raichu` (`priority 0`) was the *first* action in the `0`-priority bracket after `p2b: Raichu`'s `+3` `Fake Out`. Because `Fake Out` (`priority +3`) did not share `x.priority` (`0`), `between()` refused to match `Ninetales`, even though any move of `Ninetales` with priority `> +3` (`Protect`) would have resolved before `Fake Out` (`+3`), and `Ninetales` has no negative-priority moves — proving its priority was `0` and `Spe(Ninetales) >= Spe(p1a: Raichu)`.
