1. **Showdown's Behavior**:
   In `pokemon-showdown/sim/battle.ts` (lines 2781–2798):

   Showdown queues an automatic `instaswitch` **only** if `action.target.position < action.pokemon.side.active.length` (i.e. if the revived Pokémon was previously active in a slot that was left unoccupied).
   When a Pokémon was revived to the bench (as Indeedee was, since Sinistcha had already replaced it on Turn 1), it remains on the bench.

## 6. Why All Three Views Gave Up

### Universal Propagation Across Perspectives
`reconstruct()` is the foundation for verifying battle progression across all views:
- In `p2@p1` (channel 1), P1's sets are known, P2 is inferred.
- In `p1@p2` (channel 2), P2's sets are known, P1 is inferred.
- In `both@spectator` (channel 0), both sides are inferred.

Because `planSegment` processes the observed channel log identically across all perspectives, every perspective encounters the missing forced-switch entry on Turn 5:
1. Reconstruct cannot verify past Turn 4 (`verifiedThroughTurn = 4`).
2. When `turn 5: a choice was rejected` occurs, `reconstruct` attempts to backtrack across variants:
   - In one variant, it changes Dragonite's target from `+2` (Pawmot) to `+1` (Delphox). Pawmot does not faint, avoiding the upkeep switch, but this produces a hard diff against the log:
     `expected: '|move|p1a: Dragonite|Dragon Pulse|p2b: Pawmot', actual: '|move|p1a: Dragonite|Dragon Pulse|p2a: Delphox'`.
   - In channel 0 and channel 2, Sneasler's HP percentage on Turn 5 (96/100 vs 97/100) triggers die backtracks to redraw Turn 4. Every redrawn die branch where Pawmot faints hits the rejected choice on Turn 5, causing `dieBacktracks` to exhaust.

### Exhaustion of the Inference Budget
In `inferSpreads`:
1. Every round rebuilds the battle to turn 4 and fails on Turn 5.
2. Because Turn 5 is not verified, `past(at, deepest)` never advances beyond Turn 4.
3. `fallback()` is called on Turn 5 diffs. Fallback runs hundreds of rebuilds and thousands of probes trying candidate spreads across the unknown Pokémon.
4. Because the failure is an unhandled protocol choice in `reconstruct.mjs` and not a stat incompatibility, no possible EV spread can allow the simulator to accept `pass, pass` on Turn 5 upkeep.
5. Limits are hit sequentially:
   - `guessTries` exhausted during candidate selection.
   - `fallbackRebuilds` and `probes` exhausted in fallback search.
   - `backtracks` exhausted within individual rebuild passes.
6. When the search cannot advance, `inferSpreads` gives up on the search (`stop = "searches"`).
7. `inferSpreads` initiates its restart hierarchy (`restarts()` in `infer.mjs`):
   - **Restart 1 (Usage)**: Re-runs the entire search starting from usage priors (`scripts/fixtures/usage-spreads.json`).
   - **Restart 2 (Wide)**: Re-runs with 4× fallback rebuilds and joint guess tries.
   - **Restart 3 (Widest)**: Re-runs all starts with all limits scaled up by 4× (`budgetScale * 4`).
8. Across all restarts and passes, the search hits the exact same roadblock on Turn 5:
   - `p2@p1`: 48 rounds, 206 rebuilds, 71,460 probes (3,598.6s / ~1.0h).
   - `p1@p2`: 44 rounds, 220 rebuilds, 67,411 probes (3,607.7s / ~1.0h).
   - `both@spectator`: 86 rounds, 372 rebuilds, 114,848 probes (10,029.5s / ~2.79h).
     Spectator view took nearly 3 hours because both sides are unknown, expanding the joint candidate search space across 8 Pokémon and multiplying the number of passes and fallback probes executed before reaching `stop = "searches"`.
