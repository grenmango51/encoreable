## 5. Root Cause Analysis: Why `p1@p2` Gave Up

### A. The Simulator's Die Collision in Turn 5
In `scripts/lib/reconstruct.mjs`, when reconstructing without the battle seed (S4 mode), the simulation begins from `startSeed = sampler(sampleSeed ^ 0x5eed).seed()`:
1. Under `startSeed` with default or usage guesses, the random draw sequence reaches Turn 5 where Sneasler executes Rock Slide.
2. At die index `i = 68` (the 30% flinch check on Basculegion), `startSeed` produces `value = 0` ($0 < 30$).
3. This causes Basculegion to flinch, printing:
   ```text
   |cant|p1b: Basculegion|flinch
   ```
   instead of the expected line:
   ```text
   |move|p1b: Basculegion|Muddy Water|p2a: Sneasler|[spread] p2a,p2b
   ```
4. This mismatch triggers `resolveTurn(5, ...)` in `reconstruct.mjs`.

### B. The Single-Die Local Search Trap in `resolveTurn`
In `reconstruct.mjs`, `resolveTurn` searches for random draw substitutions by iterating over draws in `order` and testing candidate values one die at a time:
- When `resolveTurn` evaluates die 68, candidates are generated: `candidates({ i: 68, lo: 0, hi: 99, value: 0, chance: false })` returns `[99]`.
- Testing `subs[68] = 99` successfully prevents Basculegion from flinching. Basculegion proceeds to execute `Muddy Water`.
- **However, subsequent dice in the PRNG stream are uncoordinated**:
  - Specifically, at die index 73 (Muddy Water's critical hit check on Sneasler), `startSeed` produces `value = 0` (a 1/24 critical hit).
  - This critical hit inflicts massive damage on Sneasler, knocking Sneasler out immediately on Turn 5:
    ```text
    |-crit|p2a: Sneasler
    |-damage|p2a: Sneasler|0 fnt
    |faint|p2a: Sneasler
    ```
  - But the observed log demands:
    ```text
    |-damage|p2a: Sneasler|5/157
    ```
- Because Sneasler takes a critical hit and faints, the probe with `subs[68] = 99` **still diverges at the exact same hard line index** (`firstDivergence` returns index 6 in both cases).
- The ranking metric in `scoreOf(run, t)` evaluates:
  ```javascript
  const reach = rec.score;
  if (outranks(reach, best)) { ... }
  ```
  Because substituting die 68 alone produces an immediate divergence on Sneasler's damage line, `outranks(reach, best)` is **false**.
- The algorithm cannot see that die 68 is the correct prerequisite die without simultaneously substituting die 73 (no crit) and die 74 (damage roll 64).
- Because no single candidate die improves the turn score, `resolveTurn` falls back to `fallback`:
  ```javascript
  if (!committed) {
    if (!fallback) { if (!wide) { wide = true; continue; } break; }
    commit(fallback.i, fallback.value);
  }
  ```
  `fallback` commits `{ i: 68, value: 0 }` (the flinch).
- Turn 5 is abandoned as failing with `|cant|p1b: Basculegion|flinch`.

### C. Rigid Constraints of `p1@p2` (Known Opponent Side + Exact Integer HP)
In `p1@p2`:
1. **Opponent Side is Fully Known**: Player 2's sets are marked `known[1] = true`. Their Stat Points, abilities, and items are fixed constants. The engine cannot adjust Player 2's Spe, Def, or HP.
2. **Exact Integer HP Reporting**: On Channel 2, Player 2's HP values are reported as exact integers:
   - Sneasler must hit exactly `5/157` (requiring exactly 64 damage from Muddy Water).
   - Froslass must hit exactly `38/154` (requiring exactly 52 damage from Muddy Water).
   - Froslass must then be knocked out to exactly `0 fnt` by Golisopod's Leech Life (requiring $\ge 38$ damage).
3. **Stat Point Fallback Ineffectiveness**:
   In `infer.mjs`, when `reconstruct` fails on Turn 5, `fallback` tests alternative Stat Point allocations for Player 1 (Basculegion's Spe, SpA, HP, Def; Golisopod's Spe, Atk, HP).
   Because every rebuild from `startSeed` flinches Basculegion at die 68 regardless of Basculegion's or Golisopod's Stat Points:
   ```text
   fallback on turn 5: Basculegion spe 0 - plays out the same
   fallback on turn 5: Basculegion spe 32 - stops at 5.6: wanted |move|... got |cant|...
   ```

## 7. Contrast: Why `p2@p1` and `both@spectator` Succeeded

### Why `p2@p1` Succeeded in 60.4s
1. **Opponent Side is Unknown**: In `p2@p1`, Player 1 is known and Player 2 is unknown. Player 2's Stat Points are free variables explored by fallback.
2. **Speed-Tie PRNG Stream Shift**:
   In Round 5, fallback for `p2@p1` explored candidate Stat Points for Player 2's Pokémon:
   ```text
   fallback on turn 5: Arcanine spe 9 - reproduces it, 13.5s
   fallback on turn 5: reproduced in 7 rebuilds, 22.8s
   round 6, 1.8s: the whole log reproduces
   ```
   Setting Arcanine to 9 Spe gave Arcanine exactly 130 Speed (matching Basculegion's 130 Speed), creating a Speed tie in early turns.
3. This speed tie consumed an additional PRNG draw, shifting the PRNG alignment by exactly 1 draw when entering Turn 5.
4. Under this shifted PRNG alignment, die 105 (the flinch check) naturally rolled `35` ($35 \ge 30$, no flinch), and the subsequent Muddy Water roll did not crit Sneasler, cleanly reproducing Turn 5 through Turn 8 in Round 6.

### Why `both@spectator` Succeeded in 1,362.4s
1. **Both Sides are Unknown Variables**: In `both@spectator`, neither Player 1 nor Player 2 is pinned to fixed stats. The joint guess picker and fallback can adjust Stat Points on both sides simultaneously.
2. **Coarse Percentage Tolerance on Channel 0**:
   - In Channel 0 (spectator view), HP values are reported as rounded percentages: Sneasler is `3/100` (instead of exact `5/157`), and Froslass is `24/100` (instead of exact `38/154`).
   - The wider tolerance of percentage brackets allows damage rolls that would fail exact integer checks on Channel 2 to be accepted on Channel 0.
3. Over 55 rounds, 163 rebuilds, and 122,189 probes, `both@spectator` found a joint assignment of Stat Points across both sides that avoided the Turn 5 single-die flinch/crit deadlock and successfully verified all 8 turns.

## 8. Summary of Findings
1. **Failure Locus**: The inference in `p1@p2` failed because the reconstruction engine could not progress past Turn 4 / Turn 5.
2. **Mechanistic Barrier**: Turn 5 contains a multi-die collision under the simulator's PRNG start seed:
   - Die 68 rolls a flinch on Basculegion (`value = 0`).
   - Resolving die 68 to no flinch (`value = 99`) causes the immediately subsequent Muddy Water roll (die 73) to critically strike and prematurely knock out Sneasler.
   - Because single-die substitutions in `resolveTurn` evaluate one die at a time, neither die 68 alone nor die 73 alone improves the turn divergence score, causing `resolveTurn` to commit the flinch.
3. **Information Asymmetry**: `p1@p2` failed specifically *because* Player 2 was known. In `p2@p1` and `both@spectator`, Player 2's Stat Points were free variables that could shift the PRNG draw alignment through speed tiers or meet broader percentage tolerances, whereas in `p1@p2`, Player 2's exact stats and exact integer HP requirements strictly locked the simulation into the Turn 5 deadlock.
4. **Stopping Condition**: All search budgets across default, usage, wide, and widest restarts were exhausted without advancing beyond Turn 4, terminating with `stop = "searches"` and leaving Golisopod at 136.7M unconstrained spreads.
