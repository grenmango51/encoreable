## 7. Root Cause Analysis & Deep Failure Diagnosis

### 1. The Stochastic Secondary Effect Trap (Muddy Water 30% Accuracy Drop)
Muddy Water is an area-of-effect Water move with an independent 30% probability of dropping the target's accuracy stage by 1.
- In Showdown, secondary effect checks consume a PRNG die call: `this.random(100) < move.secondary.chance`.
- In the ground truth mimic battle, the random die rolled $> 30$, resulting in zero accuracy drop lines.
- In candidate reconstruction simulations, differences in prior PRNG consumption (or speed tie resolutions earlier in the battle) phase-shifted the PRNG seed sequence.
- Consequently, on Turn 4, Showdown consistently rolled $\le 30$, triggering `|-unboost|p2b: Sneasler|accuracy|1`.
- Because the ground truth expected `|upkeep`, this produced a persistent, unresolvable protocol mismatch:
  ```
  wanted |upkeep, got |-unboost|p2b: Sneasler|accuracy|1
  ```

### 2. The Fallback Abort Mechanism (`infer.mjs` Line 614)

7. Line 484 marks the diff as permanently fruitless (`spend.fruitless.add(mark)`).
8. Fallback never runs its single-stat coordinate probes on Turn 4, completely paralyzing the search engine.

### 3. Drainage Calculation Coupling & Off-By-One HP Boundary
The drain formula in Pokémon Showdown calculates healed HP as:
$$\text{healedHP} = \left\lceil \frac{\text{damageDealt}}{2} \right\rceil$$
The percentage emitted to spectator channel 0 is:
$$\text{pct} = \left\lfloor \frac{100 \times (\text{currentHP} + \text{healedHP})}{\text{maxHP}} \right\rfloor$$
This formula creates an extremely brittle nonlinear step function:
- A change of just 1 single HP point in damage dealt (or 1 point in Eelektross's max HP) flips the percentage between `60/100` and `61/100`.
- In spectator mode, neither Eelektross's true max HP nor Indeedee's true max HP is known.
- Because `base` halted on this 1% step-function boundary, fallback was prevented from ever reaching Turn 4 to diagnose the secondary effect unboost.

### 4. Breakdown of Sequential Candidate Pinning
Because fallback returned `none()` and could provide no directional pruning:
- `choose()` was forced to guess candidate spreads blind from remaining pools containing tens of millions of combinations ($10^7 - 10^8$).
- Every random candidate assignment repeatedly reproduced the identical failure mode: either failing at Turn 2.13 on drain healing or reaching Turn 4 and hitting the Muddy Water accuracy drop.
- Across all 4 restart levels (`default`, `usage`, `wide`, and `widest`), `newRounds` remained 0, triggering the final stopping condition:
  $$\text{stop} = \text{"searches"}$$

### 5. Architectural Recommendations
1. **Tolerant Fallback Pre-Roll**:
   When `fallback(diff)` evaluates `base = await rebuild(start, budget)` on turn $t$, if `reach(base)[0] < t`, fallback should not instantly abort with `none()`. Instead, it should allow a small backtrack budget (`maxBacktracks = 2`) during the pre-roll to reconcile off-by-one percentage quantization differences on earlier turns.
2. **Stochastic Secondary Effect Branching**:
   Secondary effects with low probabilities (such as Muddy Water's 30% accuracy drop) should be treated as branch points in reconstruct. When a replay protocol shows `|upkeep` rather than an expected secondary effect line, the die search should explicitly constrain the secondary effect die roll to $> \text{chance}$, avoiding desynchronization.
