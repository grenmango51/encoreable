### Technical Takeaways & Engine Implications
1. **Coupled Multi-Target Spread Moves as Combinatorial Bottlenecks**:
   When a spread move (Floette's Dazzling Gleam, Hatterene's Dazzling Gleam, Camerupt's Heat Wave) hits two targets simultaneously under spectator view, the simulator generates two independent ceiling percentage lines. Any candidate spread pair for the attacker and the two defenders must satisfy both independent intervals simultaneously. When combined with Follow Me redirection and subsequent Flare Blitz recoil, the joint feasible volume in 6-dimensional SP space shrinks by orders of magnitude.
2. **Extreme Sensitivity of Threshold-Triggered Items (Sitrus Berry)**:
   Incineroar's health sequence on Turn 2 showcases the fragility of threshold-dependent items in spectator inference. Incineroar must take enough damage from Hatterene's Dazzling Gleam to land at $51\%$, strictly avoiding the $\le 50.0\%$ Sitrus trigger, but then must take enough recoil from Flare Blitz to drop to $37\%$, successfully triggering the Sitrus Berry. A single stat perturbation that shifts the incoming damage by even 1% either triggers Sitrus Berry too early (desynchronizing the item log) or misses the recoil window downstream.
3. **Failure of Single-Parameter Fallback Perturbation**:
   The fallback heuristic in `infer.mjs` perturbs one stat of one Pokémon at a time (e.g. `Incineroar hp 32`, `Incineroar atk 14`, `Floette hp 21`). In tightly coupled exchanges like Turns 1–3, shifting Incineroar's HP to satisfy Turn 3 Heat Wave inherently shifts Turn 1 Flare Blitz recoil and Turn 2 Sitrus Berry threshold. A 1-D coordinate descent cannot navigate the narrow diagonal manifold of coupled attacker/defender bulk; joint multi-stat perturbation or interval-based propagation across verified prefixes is required to escape this cyclic stalling.
