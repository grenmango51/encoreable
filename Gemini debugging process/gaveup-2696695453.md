## 6. The Anatomy of Search Failure in `both@spectator`

In `both@spectator`, neither player's sets or stats are known (`known = [false, false]`). The search is exposed to four severe mathematical traps that completely blocked candidate generation:

### Trap 1: The Dire Claw PRNG Branching Trap (`wanted par, got slp`)
Mega Golisopod and Sneasler introduce non-deterministic secondary effect rolls into Showdown's PRNG stream. On Turn 2:
```
move | p1b: Sneasler | Dire Claw | p2a: Arcanine
```
In Showdown (`sim/battle-actions.ts` / `data/moves.ts`), Dire Claw executes:
1. `this.random(100) < 50` (50% secondary effect trigger)
2. `this.random(3)` (0: poison, 1: paralysis, 2: sleep)

In the real mimic battle, this call sequence produced **Paralysis**:
```
|-status|p2a: Arcanine|par
```
However, in spectator inference:
- The exact PRNG state entering Turn 2 depends strictly on the exact number of PRNG calls made on Turn 1 (Volt Switch damage roll, Dragon Pulse damage roll, and speed tie checks if any).
- When a candidate guess possessed even a minor discrepancy in Rotom's SpA, Persian's SpD, or Garchomp's stats, the damage calculation rounded differently, or speed checks altered the internal PRNG frame count.
- Consequently, on Turn 2, the status roll landed on **Sleep**:
```
wanted |-status|p2a: Arcanine|par, got |-status|p2a: Arcanine|slp
```
Because the engine verifies spectator protocol strictly line-by-line, the status divergence rejected the guess immediately at engine line `2.13`.

- Phase 2: Rounds 1, 2, 3, 4, 5

- Phase 4: Rounds 5, 6, 7, 8
- Phase 5: Rounds 1, 2, 3, 4, 5

### Trap 2: The Rotom-Heat Speed Desync & Fallback Perturbations
To bypass `wanted par, got slp`, the fallback perturbation algorithm systematically modified individual stats of active Pokémon:
