### The Turn 3 / Turn 4 Speed-Tie Order Inversion Trap

The failure in `both@spectator` is caused by an undetectable speed-tie order inversion between two allied Pokémon on the same side of the field:

1. **The Observed Action Order in Ground Truth Protocol**:
   - **Turn 3**:
     - `p2b: Salamence` moves **before** `p2a: Sneasler`:
       Line 3.7: `|move|p2b: Salamence|Hyper Voice|p1a: Sylveon|[spread] p1a,p1b`
       Line 3.10: `|move|p2a: Sneasler|Rock Slide|p1b: Garchomp|[spread] p1a`
   - **Turn 4**:
     - `p2a: Sneasler` moves **before** `p2b: Salamence`:
       Line 4.5: `|move|p2a: Sneasler|Rock Slide|p1a: Sylveon|[spread] p1a,p1b`
       Line 4.8: `|move|p2b: Salamence|Draco Meteor|p1a: Sylveon`

2. **The Mathematical Implication**:
   - Mega Salamence has base Speed 120 and Timid (+Spe) nature.
   - Sneasler has base Speed 120 and Jolly (+Spe) nature.
   - Level 50 Speed formulas:
     $$\text{Spe}_{\text{Salamence}} = \lfloor (120 + SP_{\text{spe}, \text{Sala}} + 20) \times 1.1 \rfloor = \lfloor (140 + SP_{\text{spe}, \text{Sala}}) \times 1.1 \rfloor$$
     $$\text{Spe}_{\text{Sneasler}} = \lfloor (120 + SP_{\text{spe}, \text{Sneas}} + 20) \times 1.1 \rfloor = \lfloor (140 + SP_{\text{spe}, \text{Sneas}}) \times 1.1 \rfloor$$
   - Between Turn 3 and Turn 4:
     - No Tailwind, Trick Room, Paralysis, or Speed-stage modifications occurred.
     - Sneasler's White Herb was not consumed (Unburden was inactive).
     - Neither Pokémon gained or lost Speed.
   - If their speeds were deterministic and unequal:
     - If $\text{Spe}_{\text{Sala}} > \text{Spe}_{\text{Sneas}}$, Salamence must move first on both Turn 3 and Turn 4.
     - If $\text{Spe}_{\text{Sneas}} > \text{Spe}_{\text{Sala}}$, Sneasler must move first on both Turn 3 and Turn 4.
   - **Therefore, the order reversal between Turn 3 and Turn 4 is mathematically impossible unless**:
     $$\mathbf{Speed(Mega\ Salamence) = Speed(Sneasler)}$$
     and the order was decided by a random 50/50 PRNG speed tie-breaker on both turns!

### Why Coordinate Fallback Is Trapped in an Infinite Cycle

When the inference engine attempts to resolve this discrepancy in `both@spectator`, coordinate fallback (`fallback()` in `infer.mjs`) is invoked:
1. **Single-Coordinate Perturbation Architecture**:
   - `fallback(seen, start, ...)` perturbs **one stat of one Pokémon at a time** along the Stat Point lattice.
2. **The Turn 3 Patch**:
   - On Turn 3, when candidate guesses produced Sneasler moving first (Round 5), fallback adjusted `Salamence spe` to 16 (Spe 171) while Sneasler had `spe 15` (Spe 170).
   - This made Mega Salamence deterministically 1 point faster than Sneasler ($171 > 170$).
   - Fallback reported: `fallback on turn 3: Salamence spe 16 - reproduces it, 176.2s (in 30 rebuilds)`.
   - Turn 3 reproduced successfully!
3. **The Turn 4 Contradiction**:
   - However, with Salamence's Speed fixed at 171 and Sneasler's at 170, the simulation entered Turn 4.
   - On Turn 4, the ground truth wanted Sneasler to move first (`wanted Rock Slide`).
   - But because Salamence was deterministically faster, Salamence moved first (`got Draco Meteor`).
   - The rebuild failed on line 4.5.
