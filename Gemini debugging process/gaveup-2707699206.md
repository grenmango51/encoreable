## 5. Diagnostic Investigation: Why `both@spectator` Stalled and Gave Up

### A. The Symmetrical Intimidate Lead Deadlock (Turn 0)
Both players led Incineroar in slot b. In competitive doubles, Incineroar is ubiquitous (usage rank 1). Because both have identical base Speed (60) and identical Careful natures, their level 50 Speed ranges are identical:
$$\text{Speed} \in [80, 112]$$
Showdown triggers simultaneous entry abilities strictly in descending Speed order (resolving ties via RNG).
In the mimic battle, `p1b: Incineroar` triggered Intimidate first. Any fallback perturbation that increased `p2b: Incineroar spe` above `p1b: Incineroar` immediately broke line `0.22`. This locked `p2b: Incineroar spe` to $\le \text{Spe}(\text{p1b: Incineroar})$, eliminating a large portion of the parameter space before Turn 1 was even reached.

### B. The Pre-Mega Speed Inversion Trap (Turn 1)
Delphox (base Spe 104, Timid) and Gengar (base Spe 110, Timid) both Mega Evolved on Turn 1.
Because Showdown determines Mega Evolution animation order by pre-Mega Speed, and Delphox must move first in the log, Delphox's pre-Mega Speed must strictly exceed Gengar's pre-Mega Speed:
$$\text{Spe}(\text{Delphox}) > \text{Spe}(\text{Gengar})$$
At level 50 Timid:
- Delphox Spe: $\lfloor (104 + \text{SP} + 20) \times 1.1 \rfloor \in [136, 171]$
- Gengar Spe: $\lfloor (110 + \text{SP} + 20) \times 1.1 \rfloor \in [143, 178]$
To satisfy this inequality, Delphox must carry heavy Speed investment ($\text{SP} \ge 15$, Spe $\ge 152$) while Gengar must carry low Speed investment ($\text{SP} \le 8$, Spe $\le 151$).
Whenever fallback attempted to alter Delphox's or Gengar's Speed to influence subsequent turns, this strict inequality was violated, causing 8 separate fallback rebuilds to abort at line `1.0`.

### C. The Discrete OHKO Bulk Boundary (Turn 1)
Delphox's Psyshock targets physical Defense. Gengar (base HP 60, base Def 60) is exceptionally frail physically:
- Level 50 Mega Gengar HP: $135 + \text{SP}_{\text{HP}}$
- Level 50 Mega Gengar Def: $\lfloor (80 + \text{SP}_{\text{Def}} + 20) \times 1.0 \rfloor = 100 + \text{SP}_{\text{Def}}$
In the ground truth battle, Psyshock dealt an exact OHKO (`0 fnt`).
Whenever fallback increased Gengar's Defense or HP (`Gengar def 29, 30, 32`), Gengar survived with 15%–17% HP (`stops at 1.8`). This locked Gengar's Defense SP to $\le 28$.

### D. The Revival Blessing Parity Constraint (Turn 3)
Pawmot's Revival Blessing restores exactly half of the target's Max HP, rounded down:
$$\text{RevivedHP} = \left\lfloor \frac{\text{MaxHP}}{2} \right\rfloor$$
Spectator Channel 0 displays HP as an integer floor percentage:
$$\text{DisplayPct} = \left\lfloor 100 \times \frac{\text{RevivedHP}}{\text{MaxHP}} \right\rfloor$$
If Gengar's Max HP is even ($2k$):
$$\frac{k}{2k} = 0.50 \implies 50\%$$
If Gengar's Max HP is odd ($2k + 1$):
$$\frac{k}{2k + 1} < 0.50 \implies 49\%$$
Because the spectator log asserts `50/100y`, Gengar's Max HP MUST be an even integer. Since Base HP is 60 and IV is 31, level 50 HP is:
$$\text{MaxHP} = 60 + \text{SP}_{\text{HP}} + 75 = 135 + \text{SP}_{\text{HP}}$$
For Max HP to be even, $\text{SP}_{\text{HP}}$ must be **odd** ($1, 3, 5, \dots, 31$).
Whenever fallback assigned an even HP value (`Gengar hp 0, 16, 32`), Revival Blessing restored $49/100$, immediately aborting rebuilds at line `3.5`.

### E. Multi-Target Spread Drain Coupling (Turns 2, 3, 4)

$$\text{DrainHeal} = \left\lfloor \frac{\text{Damage}}{2} \right\rfloor$$
On Turn 2, Matcha Gotcha strikes both Basculegion and Incineroar.
On Turn 3, Matcha Gotcha strikes Incineroar.
On Turn 4, Matcha Gotcha strikes Incineroar again.
At each turn, spectator Channel 0 observes:
1. Basculegion damage percentage (`9/100`).
2. Incineroar damage percentage (`63/100`, `54/100`).
3. Sinistcha cumulative drain healing percentage (`58/100`, `64/100`, `69/100`).
Because drain healing accumulates damage dealt across both targets and divides by Sinistcha's Max HP, Sinistcha's displayed percentage is simultaneously coupled to:
- Sinistcha's SpA and Max HP.
- Basculegion's SpD and Max HP.

## 6. Comparison: Single-View vs. Spectator Inference

### Why `p1@p2` and `p2@p1` Succeeded in <35 Seconds
1. **Uncoupling of the System of Equations**:
   - In `p1@p2` (Player 2 observer), Player 2's entire roster (`Gengar`, `Incineroar`, `Basculegion`, `Pawmot`, `Altaria`, `Rillaboom`) is known ground truth.
   - P2 Incineroar's exact Speed (80), Defense (110), SpD (121), and Max HP (170) are known constants.
   - Gengar's exact Max HP, pre-Mega Speed, and bulk are known constants.
   - Basculegion's exact Scarf Speed (147) and Max HP (195) are known constants.
   - Consequently, Player 1's unknown stats (Delphox Speed/SpA, Sinistcha bulk/SpA) solve as independent, uncoupled 1D inequalities against fixed constants.
2. **Exact Integer HP Numerators**:
   - In `p2@p1` (Player 1 observer), Player 1's entire roster (`Delphox`, `Incineroar`, `Sinistcha`, `Kingambit`, `Sneasler`, `Floette-Eternal`) is known ground truth.
   - Sinistcha's drain healing on Turns 2, 3, and 4 is observed with exact integer HP numerators on Player 1's screen (e.g. `101/146`, `112/146`), rather than rounded percentages (`64/100`, `69/100`).
   - Exact integer damage values directly pin opponent stat points without multi-point rounding ambiguity.
3. **No Target Ambiguity**:
   - In single-player views, the observer knows their own choice and receives exact network logs, completely bypassing the Parting Shot target discrepancy.
   - Both single-observer runs converged in $\le 10$ rounds, $\le 15$ rebuilds, and $<3,800$ probes.

### Why `both@spectator` Failed
- In `both@spectator`, neither player's team sheet is known (`known = [false, false]`).
- All 12 parameters across the 7 active Pokémon are unknown variables.
- All damage and healing events are observed through rounded percentages $\lfloor 100 \times \frac{\text{HP}}{\text{MaxHP}} \rfloor$.
- Symmetrical lead Incineroars, overlapping pre-Mega speeds, discrete Psyshock OHKO boundaries, odd/even Max HP parity constraints on Revival Blessing, and multi-target spread drain healing created an intricately coupled non-linear system.
- When Turn 4 diverged at Line 4.9 on Parting Shot target execution, fallback coordinate descent could not bridge the gap without breaking an earlier turn's assertions.

### Primary Root Causes
1. **The Turn 4 Parting Shot Target Deadlock**:
   At Line 4.9, the ground truth mimic replay executed `|move|p2a: Incineroar|Parting Shot|p1b: Sinistcha`. In candidate rebuilds, Showdown's target resolution or die state executed `|move|p2a: Incineroar|Parting Shot|p1a: Delphox`. Because fallback operates with univariate Stat Point mutation, it could not alter the discrete target choice without perturbing stats that broke earlier turns.
2. **Interlocking Speed-Tier and Parity Gridlock**:
   The match contained three rigid sequential thresholds:
   - Turn 0: $\text{Spe}(\text{p1b: Incineroar}) \ge \text{Spe}(\text{p2b: Incineroar})$ (Intimidate trigger order).
   - Turn 1: $\text{Pre-Mega Spe}(\text{p1a: Delphox}) > \text{Pre-Mega Spe}(\text{p2a: Gengar})$ (Mega Evolution animation order).
   - Turn 3: $\text{MaxHP}(\text{Gengar}) \equiv 0 \pmod 2$ (Revival Blessing 50% vs 49% heal).
   Any coordinate descent step that violated any of these three instantly failed at line `0.22`, `1.0`, or `3.5`.
3. **Multi-Target Spread Drain Quantization**:
   Sinistcha's Matcha Gotcha spread drain healing on Turns 2, 3, and 4 coupled Sinistcha's bulk and SpA simultaneously to Basculegion's and Incineroar's defensive stats. Under coarse spectator percentage rounding, univariate perturbations could not find a joint equilibrium.
4. **Exhaustion of All Search Tiers**:
   The engine cycled through all 6 restart tiers (`default`, `usage`, `wide`, `widest default`, `widest usage`, `widest wide`), exhausted 128 fallback rebuilds, and hit all backtrack, probe, and search level limits, terminating cleanly and soundly with `stop = "searches"`.
