## 7. Conclusion & Architectural Insights

1. **Why `p1@p2` Gave Up in the Cluster Sweep**:
   `p1@p2` failed not because of a simulator bug, but because the candidate spread space for Player 1 was heavily coupled across multiple complex mechanics:
   - Four separate Grassy Terrain healing cycles and three weather transitions (Sun $\rightarrow$ Rain $\rightarrow$ Sun).
   - Three independent recoil events on Incineroar, where each recoil value depended on the exact residual HP of the defending target.
   - A critical three-way Speed tie/near-tie between Incineroar (Spe 72), Grimmsnarl (Spe 72), and Kingambit (Spe 71).
   Without exact ground-truth HP numerators on Player 1's side, integer percentage rounding ($1\%$ ambiguity $\approx 2$ HP) caused candidate spreads to drift, triggering search level escalation and exhausting the search budget (`stop = "searches"` after 4,120.7s).

2. **The Fragility of Fallback on Single Stats**:
   The trace log highlights a recurring vulnerability in `fallback()`: when a line fails due to an RNG accuracy event (such as Sneasler's Rock Slide missing one target) or an accumulated percentage mismatch from earlier turns, testing single-stat extremes (`Sneasler atk 0`, `Grimmsnarl hp 0`, `Grimmsnarl atk 32`) inevitably breaks earlier verified damage lines (Turns 2, 5, or 7), burning the 8-rebuild fallback budget with zero forward progress.

3. **The Decisive Power of Usage Priors**:
   In `both@spectator`, the default search gave up at Round 10. The immediate restart from usage priors succeeded because 7 of the 8 active Pokémon in this match ran standard Smogon usage spreads. Once seeded with usage priors, the search converged in 423 seconds, successfully reproducing the battle with zero real spreads pruned and zero broken proofs.
